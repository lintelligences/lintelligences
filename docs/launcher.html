▸ 01 · Theoretical Grounding

Behaviors Driven by Curiosity vs. Activity

This behavioral model started from an external question in network neuroscience and philosophy: what does human curiosity look like as a trajectory through knowledge space? In a series of Wikipedia studies led by Dani S. Bassett, Perry Zurn, David M. Lydon-Staley, and Dale Zhou,[1] readers were modeled as walking across article graphs in three kinesthetic styles—the focused Hunter pursuing tight local targets, the nomadic Busybody sampling loose threads of novelty, and the creative Dancer taking heavy-tailed leaps to bridge distant domains.

Science Advances · Oct 2024
Architectural styles of curiosity in global Wikipedia mobile app readership
D. Zhou, S. Patankar, D. M. Lydon-Staley, P. Zurn, M. Gerlach & D. S. Bassett

Scaled naturalistic trajectory analysis to roughly 482,000 Wikipedia mobile readers across 50 countries and 14 languages, empirically confirming the Dancer—heavy-tailed leaps bridging disparate semantic clusters alongside Hunter and Busybody walks.

doi:10.1126/sciadv.adn3268 ↗

Bringing that taxonomy to everyday computing shifts the object of study from behaviors driven purely by curiosity to behaviors driven by activity. Wikipedia readers walk a single encyclopedia graph for open-ended consumption; active work spans source files, personal notes, and web pages in service of something being built or untangled, where excursions out for scent routinely return to a home patch.[2] Tracking those paths is useful for search and context re-entry, which this essay focuses on, and for quieter questions left mostly out of scope here—among them how emotional and cognitive states supervene on, or leave legible signatures in, behavioral states.

▸ 02 · Search Behavior

Meeting Attention Halfway: Instant Recall vs. Agentic Steering

Stepping inside someone's active train of thought makes search deceptively hard to get right. If you roughly remember a file or note, waiting four seconds for an LLM agent on the first keystroke breaks your stride. Yet pure keyword matching fails when you only recall the shape of an idea, pure vector search fails when you need an exact function signature or "what was I reading late Tuesday night?", and raw similarity scores across lopsided channels (nearly ten thousand noisy browser visits vs. four hundred curated notes) are impossible to compare directly.

The search layer addresses both constraints by pairing per-channel Reciprocal Rank Fusion[3] (score = weight / (60 + rank)) with a two-gear Human-in-the-Loop LangGraph state machine—giving instant deterministic recall up front, and waking a local tool-routing model only when you push back:

▸ 03 · Semantic Geometry

Meaningful Semantic Geometry Across Channels

Before a trajectory across code, notes, and web pages can mean anything geometrically, heterogeneous channels have to share an honest distance metric. Background watchers index edits in ~/PROJECTS, Markdown notes in Obsidian, and visited pages in Chrome's SQLite history into partitioned LanceDB tables. Multi-chunk files are collapsed via zero-copy PyArrow scans to one event per source—timestamp = max(modified_at), vector = mean(chunk_vectors)—so saving a long Python file does not masquerade as two hundred consecutive steps.

Early clustering exposed a structural bias in off-the-shelf text encoders: raw embeddings organize mixed corpora by syntax and medium rather than subject. Measuring Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) against topic groupings showed raw file vectors clustering overwhelmingly by programming language (ARI ≈ 0.74)—two unrelated Python scripts sit at 0.83 cosine similarity simply because both contain def and import.

To make distance reflect meaning across channels, the indexer applies group centroid residualization: subtracting the rolling mean vector of each file extension (.py, .js, .rs, .toml) and channel (obsidian, web) prior to L2 re-normalization. Once medium bias is projected out, a Python FastAPI route, its JavaScript canvas renderer, a scratch note, and an arXiv tab co-locate around the idea they share.

▸ 04 · Behavioral Trajectories

What Work Modes Look Like as Paths Through Space

Conventional time-trackers only record which application held focus: three hours in an editor, forty minutes in Chrome. They cannot distinguish methodical debugging from yak-shaving, or aimless tab-drifting from targeted reference gathering.

In residualized space, activity streams split naturally at adaptive rhythm breaks (the 90th percentile of inter-event gaps, clamped to 5–60 minutes) into kinematic trajectories scored on nine features: median step size (local_step), near-step stickiness (pct_near), trailing-window radius (breadth), visited global clusters (unique_clusters), heavy-tailed leap ratio (jumpiness), jump frequency (jump_rate), hub-and-spoke return loops (has_return), and cross-channel transition and jump rates.

Unlike textbook synthetic blobs, real behavioral segments form a continuous, sinuous manifold—with dense spines, ragged filaments, and overlapping seams where one mode grades into the next, plus small detached islands of deterministic machine noise (such as single-domain login funnels or bulk file indexing). Across 549 segments, PCA extracted three orthogonal axes capturing 67% of variance—Locomotion (33%), Channel Mixing (19%), and Complexity & Return (15%)—which modal K-Means partitions into recurring regimes:

Regime Kinematic Signature What It Looks Like in Practice
Deep Focus High pct_near, tight breadth, near-zero channel switching Intensive close-range work inside a single module or bug reproduction.
Narrow Attention Small steady steps, low jumpiness, adjacent notes/files Close reading, steady refactoring, or linear note synthesis.
Forage & Return High unique_clusters + deterministic has_return loops Hub-and-spoke: leap out to docs, papers, or reference repos and snap back to home base.
Exploration Wide breadth, large steps, high cross_channel_rate, low return Surveying unfamiliar territory across browser tabs, notes, and prototypes.
General Work Moderate step size, balanced spread & mixing The everyday bridge region along the interior of the behavioral continuum.
Deterministic Outliers Collapsed breadth (Linear Web Flow) or zero-gap bursts (Bulk Index) OAuth/checkout funnels or automated repo syncs—cleanly isolated from cognitive work.
▸ 05 · Primary Insights

Where Activity Trajectories Break the Curiosity Simplex

Comparing empirical activity sessions against the Wikipedia curiosity framework surfaced several structural surprises alongside day-to-day awareness of longitudinal habits (how I work) and live state (how I'm working right now):

  • Simplex collapse along locomotion: For a ternary simplex to span two dimensions cleanly, its three vertices have to reflect roughly orthogonal behavioral axes. Correlating classical H / B / D scores against the PCA decomposition showed that Locomotion (PC1, 33%) is the dominant separator mapping sessions onto the original curiosity styles—pulling sticky Hunter bouts to one vertex while bundling Busybody and Dancer together at the high-locomotion end, collapsing real sessions onto a diagonal ridge.
  • Transverse axes of activity across channels: What separates high-locomotion bouts in practice lives on the two orthogonal axes absent from single-medium reading: Channel Mixing (PC2, 19%) across code, notes, and browser tabs, and Return Complexity (PC3, 15%). Toggle the simplex coloring in Fig 5 below to see how Forage & Return and unanchored Exploration sit directly on top of each other in H–B–D space and only separate once channel transitions and return loops enter the geometry.
  • Forager in the morning, explorer after lunch: Stacking sessions around a 24-hour Pacific clock exposed a sharp diurnal split. Morning high-locomotion bouts (09:00–11:00) are overwhelmingly Forage & Return—raiding external docs and papers only to snap back to an active codebase. After a noon dip, afternoon sessions (13:00–16:30) drop the return loop and shift into wider cross-channel Exploration, supplemented by intermittent 23:00–02:00 late-night streaks.
  • Forage-to-hunt transitions as a signal of transitioning knowledge: Across consecutive sessions on the same semantic neighborhood, transitions from Forage & Return into sticky Hunter / Deep Focus bouts mark the handoff where external material finishes consolidating into working understanding and execution pulls inward.
  • Live proprioception: Surfacing the active regime on open catches the quiet drift when a five-minute reference lookup slips from anchored Forage & Return into forty minutes of open-ended Exploration.
▸ 06 · Context Switching & Re-Entry

Dropping Back Into Cold Workflows

The steepest tax in research engineering isn't starting a project—it's re-entering a half-built mental model after two days away. Flat "recent files" lists lose the thread because half of that state lived in neighboring modules you read without saving, an Obsidian note, and two external documentation tabs.

The launcher reconstructs that working context at two scales. Across the global map, Gaussian-kernel recency density (heat = raw_heat / (raw_heat + 1.5)) keeps active topic neighborhoods warm together. Inside an individual repository, the Radial Trajectory Wheel turns recent history into a spatial re-entry map:

  • Contrastive semantic sectors: Agglomerative cosine clustering partitions the repo's residualized files into 3–8 wedges; a local LLM reads centroid snippets and assigns contrastive domain names (banning generic labels like utils or helpers).
  • Log-scaled recency orbit: Radial distance encodes age (r ∝ log(1 + age)), giving freshly touched files room to breathe near the inner ring while older untouched modules settle toward the perimeter.
  • Spiral revisits and external exit stubs: Your last ten steps thread across wedges (#1 = newest), applying spiral offsets when you revisit a hub file and dropping diamond stubs (web, notes) at the exact sector angle where you stepped outside the repo to forage.
